Understanding Evapotranspiration for Agriculture

Evapotranspiration (ET) is essential for determining your crops' water needs, combining evaporation and transpiration, measured in millimeters per hour (mm/hr). This guide helps you interpret ET levels and take appropriate actions for your farm.

Evapotranspiration Levels

  • 0 - 0.1 mm/hr: Very Low (Water needed)
  • 0.1 - 0.3 mm/hr: Low (Watering may be necessary)
  • 0.3 - 0.5 mm/hr: Optimal (Watering is on track)
  • 0.5 - 0.7 mm/hr: High (Increase watering frequency)
  • 0.7 mm/hr and above: Very High (Critical water need)

Action Steps Based on ET Levels

0 - 0.1 mm/hr: Very Low

Water crops to prevent drought stress. Check soil moisture regularly. Use mulch to conserve moisture. Consider collecting rainwater for irrigation!

0.1 - 0.3 mm/hr: Low

Water according to crop needs. Plan for rainfall using forecasts. Adjust irrigation times to reduce evaporation.

0.3 - 0.5 mm/hr: Optimal

Maintain current watering practices. Monitor ET levels regularly.

0.5 - 0.7 mm/hr: High

Increase watering frequency. Check for signs of heat stress.

0.7 mm/hr and above: Very High

Ensure enough water supply. Avoid planting sensitive crops during this period.
