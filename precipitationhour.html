Understanding Precipitation for Agriculture

Precipitation, measured in millimeters per hour (mm/hr), is vital for managing irrigation and crop health. This guide helps you interpret precipitation levels and take appropriate actions for your farm.

Precipitation Levels

  • 0 - 1 mm/hr: Very Low (Water as needed)
  • 1 - 2 mm/hr: Low (Monitor crop needs)
  • 2 - 5 mm/hr: Moderate (Good for crops)
  • 5 - 10 mm/hr: High (Potential for waterlogging)
  • 10 mm/hr and above: Very High (Flood risk)

Action Steps Based on Precipitation

0 - 1 mm/hr: Very Low

Water crops as needed and regularly check soil moisture. Consider using water-efficient irrigation methods to conserve water.

1 - 2 mm/hr: Low

Light rainfall. Water crops based on their specific needs and keep a close eye on weather forecasts to anticipate changes.

2 - 5 mm/hr: Moderate

Good rainfall for crops. Assess soil moisture levels and check on crop health to ensure they are benefiting from the rain without being over-saturated.

5 - 10 mm/hr: High

Heavy rainfall. Ensure proper drainage systems are working to prevent waterlogging. Watch for signs of fungal diseases that can be promoted by wet conditions.

10 mm/hr and above: Very High

Intense rainfall and potential flood risk. Evaluate fields for flooding and implement erosion control measures to protect your soil and crops.
