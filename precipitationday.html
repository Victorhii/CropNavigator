Understanding Precipitation for Agriculture

Precipitation, measured in millimeters per day (mm/day), is crucial for crop growth. This guide helps you interpret precipitation levels and take appropriate actions for your farm.

Precipitation Levels

  • 0 - 2 mm/day: Very Low (Water crops)
  • 2 - 5 mm/day: Low (Monitor water needs)
  • 5 - 10 mm/day: Moderate (Good for crops)
  • 10 - 20 mm/day: High (Prevent waterlogging)
  • 20 mm/day and above: Very High (Flood risk)

Action Steps Based on Precipitation

0 - 2 mm/day: Very Low

Crops require water. **Initiate irrigation** and check your soil moisture regularly. Consider using water-efficient systems like drip irrigation.

2 - 5 mm/day: Low

Rainfall is minimal. **Supplement with watering as needed**. This is a great time to utilize any rainwater harvesting systems you may have.

5 - 10 mm/day: Moderate

The current rainfall is beneficial for your crops. **Hold off on irrigation** and monitor soil moisture. Ensure your crops are thriving.

10 - 20 mm/day: High

Heavy rainfall is expected. **Check your field for proper drainage** to prevent waterlogging, which can damage roots. This is also a good time to check for any signs of disease promoted by wet conditions.

20 mm/day and above: Very High

There is a **significant risk of flooding**. Implement **erosion control measures** and ensure all drainage systems are clear and functioning correctly.
